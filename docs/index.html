A map you never had to build

You don't add contacts, tag anyone, or import a spreadsheet. Bob looks for one specific thing: the three-body shape where someone put you and a stranger in the same message and stepped back. That's an introduction, and it's already written down.

What comes back is a page of your own network. Every introduction Bob found, who made it, when, and a link back to the original thread for each one, so you can check any row rather than take its word for it.

How it goes

Find → See → Thank

Three commands. The first one carries the whole thing.

01
📬

Find

/bob-scan reads your mailbox and pulls out the introductions — both the ones made for you and the ones you made for other people. It knows which seat you were sitting in.

02
🕸️

See

/bob-graph draws your network as a page you can click around. Who connected you to whom, which handful of people account for most of it, and where each one traces back to.

03
💌

Thank

/bob-thanks asks what came of the introductions that took hold — a sentence each, or skip — then drafts the notes. Your sentence is the copy. Drafts only; nothing sends itself.

The details

The little things Bob does

🪑

Both seats

An introduction you made is kept apart from one made for you, because you're owed different things in each.

✅

Did it take hold?

Whether both people replied, whether anyone booked time. An introduction that happened isn't the same as one that landed.

🚪

The ones that never landed

Often the more useful list. A warm introduction going to waste is a door still open.

🔗

Every row is checkable

Each introduction links back to the original thread, so you can verify any claim Bob makes rather than trust it.

🗂️

Airtable, if you want it

Mirror what Bob found into your own base. One-way and additive — re-running never duplicates rows and never deletes anything.

✍️

Drafts, never sends

Notes land in your drafts and stay there. The credential Bob reads your mail with cannot send at all.

Worth reading before you decide

What Bob can't do

Some of these are choices and some are walls. Either way you should know them going in.

It can't tell you what came of an introduction you made

Once you hand two people off to each other, they talk to each other and not to you. Your mailbox stops being evidence at that moment. Knowing more would mean going and asking them — a different feature, not built.

It only sees one channel

Bob reads email. An introduction can land perfectly well over text, LinkedIn, or a phone call, and Bob will never know. So it asks "did it go somewhere else?" rather than telling you that you never replied.

It can't see introductions you weren't part of

The chains follow only where every step touched your own mailbox. This is a picture of your network compounding, not a map of everyone's.

It doesn't use LinkedIn, and won't

Automated LinkedIn access risks your account. That isn't a risk worth handing to anyone's tool, including this one.

It isn't a firm-wide tool

Bob is built for one person and one mailbox, so everyone would run their own. A shared view where partners see each other's introductions is a genuinely interesting problem and an honest amount of work.

It's early

Bob works and it has been run against a real six-year mailbox. But the people outside its author are only just arriving. Expect rough edges, and expect to be asked what broke.

Your data

It stays on your computer

Not as a policy you have to trust — as a fact about how it's built.

📁

A folder you pick

Two spreadsheet files and a web page, on your own machine. Open them, edit them, back them up, or delete the folder and be done.

🔒

Read-only, and yours

Bob reads your mail with a credential you create and hold. It can't send, can't delete, and can't touch anything in your account that isn't mail.

🚫

Nowhere to phone home to

There is no Bob server, no account, and no mechanism by which your data could reach anyone. Its author holds no credential of yours, because none exists to hold.

Bob will tell you when it doesn't know something. A blank field means it didn't look, never that nothing happened.
